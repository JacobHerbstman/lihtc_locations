L11@P0: Legislation Title: AN ORDINANCE relating to land use and zoning; adopting temporary
L12@P0: regulations to exempt housing projects that meet Mandatory Housing Affordability requirements
L13@P0: using on-site performance units from Design Review, and allowing permit applicants for all
L14@P0: housing subject to Full Design Review the option of complying with Design Review pursuant to
L15@P0: Administrative Design review; temporarily suspending and allowing voluntary design review of
L16@P0: proposed development in Titles 23 and 25 of the Seattle Municipal Code, consistent with
L17@P0: Chapter 333, Laws of 2023; and amending Section 23.41.004 of the Seattle Municipal Code.
L18@P0: Summary and Background of the Legislation: During the 2023 session, the State legislature
L19@P0: passed House Bill (HB) 1293, which requires Seattle and other cities and counties that have a
L20@P0: design review program to meet certain requirements.
L21@P0: HB 1293 requirements include:
L22@P0:  Using objective rather than subjective guidelines;
L23@P0:  Having only one public meeting as part of the review process;
L24@P0:  Regulating building exteriors only;
L25@P0:  Maintaining the density, height, bulk, or scale at what zoning allows; and
L26@P0:  Integrating design review into the development permit process.
L27@P0: In addition, the City Council adopted a Statement of Legislative Intent (SLI), City Council SLI
L28@P0: SDCI 4A1, in 2022. The SLI directed Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections
L29@P0: (SDCI) and Office of Planning and Community Development (OPCD) to work with a
L30@P0: stakeholder group to analyze the outcomes of Seattle’s design review program and recommend
L31@P0: best practices, and options for program modifications, including addressing barriers to equitable
L32@P0: participation. SDCI and OPCD delivered this analysis to Council in August 2024. The Mayor’s
L33@P0: Housing Subcabinet has also directed SDCI to make land use codes and permitting processes
L34@P0: simpler and more efficient, to reduce the time and cost of permitting housing.
L35@P0: SDCI is proposing amendments to the land use code to update design review to carry out state
L36@P0: law, direction from the City Council and Mayor. Carrying out these state mandates is intended to
L37@P0: update the design review program to be more efficient, better meet the current needs of the City
L38@P0: for new investment, particularly in varying and more housing options throughout the City and
L39@P0: focus the program on good design outcomes for development projects that are most likely to
L40@P0-1: impact the character of neighborhoods.Chanda Emery

L45@P1: This legislation would temporarily suspend required design review for six months, making
L46@P1: design review voluntary for proposed development. These regulations will be in place while
L47@P1: SDCI works to update the design review program and guidelines as required by ESHB 1293, as
L48@P1: well as updating affordable housing measures. These permanent changes are intended to update
L49@P1: the design review program to be more efficient; better meet the current needs of the city for new
L50@P1: investment, particularly in varying and more housing options throughout the city; and focus the
L51@P1: program on good design outcomes for developments that are most likely to impact the character
L52@P1: of neighborhoods with the highest concentrations of residents and visitors. The additional time is
L53@P1: needed for City staff to respond to the stakeholder and public engagement recommendations and
L54@P1: to work through code amendments, Director’s Rules, Tips, and guidance documents to clarify
L55@P1: code requirements and procedures. Permanent legislation and new Design Guidelines are
L56@P1: anticipated for council review following the 2025 budget process.
L57@P1: The interim suspension applies to three types of design review: Streamlined Design Review
L58@P1: (SDR), Administrative Design Review (ADR), and Full Design Review (FDR) with Design
L59@P1: Review Board (DRB) input. Design review does not apply to single-family detached residences.
L60@P1: Design review does not include life and safety reviews which are regulated by other permits and
L61@P1: other parts of the Seattle Municipal Code.
L62@P1: This legislation also reenacts temporary regulations established by Ordinance 126854 for six
L63@P1: months. The temporary regulations exempt proposed development that meet Mandatory Housing
L64@P1: Affordability requirements using on-site performance units and low-income housing from
L65@P1: Design Review, but allow the Director to approve departures for qualifying proposed
L66@P1: development as a Type I decision.
L67@P1: On September 15th the LUC amended the bill to allow projects that opt-in to design review and
L68@P1: would meet the thresholds for FDR to instead have the option of being processed under ADR,
L69@P1: and to make a technical correction. No additional budgetary impacts are anticipated.
