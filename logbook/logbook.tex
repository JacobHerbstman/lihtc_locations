\section*{Reset to new-construction project locations}
\noindent September 11, 2026. Agent-drafted research record.

Jacob chose a simple project-location dataset: accept HUD's new-construction
classification unless repeated records or a concrete inconsistency give a reason
to investigate. The new build starts from the original HUD workbook. It uses none
of the previous physical-development links or external review decisions. The prior
workflow was removed from the active project and preserved in Git history and a
pre-reset snapshot.

\begin{center}
\small
\input{reset_summary.tex}
\end{center}

The main table keeps the earliest new-construction record at each standardized
primary address. A unique record stays even when its year or hedonics are missing.
At a repeated address, a missing year or conflicting earliest-year tie prevents
selection; identical ties can use a stable representative only when their listed
characteristics agree. All raw new-construction records, including later phases,
remain in the record table. A repeated address is a review question, not proof
that a later project is a duplicate. Addresses are not parcel identifiers.

Census address-range matches provide an independent location comparison. Matches in the reported state are compared with HUD coordinates.
Non-exact strings can pass when the locations agree; without a HUD comparison,
an exact Census match is required. A discrepancy
greater than 500 meters triggers review; that screening threshold is not an error
definition. HUD-only coordinates are retained as unconfirmed. Scattered-site
projects retain their primary point without claiming that it represents every
building. Missing units or bedroom counts do not invalidate a checked location.

The remaining work is confined to repeated-address choices and unresolved
locations. There is no requirement to reconstruct rehabilitation histories or
obtain two external sources for every project. Neighborhood characteristics and
parcel matching are outside this reset. The saved Census tract codes describe
2020 geography; the analysis has not yet chosen historical neighborhood measures.

\paragraph{Reproduction.} Run \texttt{make setup}, then \texttt{make} at the
repository root. Task Makefiles are for prepared inputs; the root declares the
full dependency graph. HUD source: pinned 2024 release, retrieved August 8, 2026.
Census: Public\_AR\_ACS2025 and Census2020\_ACS2025, requests defined September 11,
2026. The code reset follows revision \texttt{fe507fd}. Saved-file reports record
rows, keys, missingness, ranges, and fingerprints. Raw geocoder responses are
retained so ordinary builds do not send the addresses again.

\section*{External checks: matching totals and specific exceptions}
\noindent September 11, 2026. Agent-drafted research record; baseline
revision \texttt{36228a3}.

The original workbook exactly matches the
\href{https://www.huduser.gov/portal/Datasets/lihtc/LIHTC-2024-Tables.pdf}{HUD 2024 summary}:
55,345 records and 3,860,546 adjusted units, including rehabilitation and
territories. All ten published annual property and unit counts for 2015--2024
also match. This establishes agreement with the source release, not the accuracy
of individual records. Our 50-state-and-DC new-construction sample uses reported
unit counts and falls from 29,453 records and 1,949,494 known units to 27,708
selected records and 1,851,152 known units. Selection removes 5.9\% of records
and 5.0\% of units. New Jersey loses 17.6\% of records and 18.5\% of units;
North Carolina loses 15.2\% and 4.9\%. These losses include unresolved ties and
later records that could be additional phases.

Three exploratory records were sampled from checked locations dated 2010--2020
(R seed 20260911). Oconee Park, Georgia (GAA20100035), is coded new construction
with 117 units in HUD. However,
\href{https://www.gao.gov/assets/a204076.html}{GAO's 2010 Georgia appendix, Table 2},
describes the same 117-unit project, also called Riverview Heights, as
rehabilitation. This is concrete evidence for an exception to HUD's default
classification. Sequoia Villas, California (CAA20120846), has 18 total units in
HUD, although its bedroom counts sum to 19. California's 2012 allocation report
and the Tulare housing authority identify 19 total units; the allocation report
separately lists 18 low-income units. Its opening year still needs confirmation.
Ohio's agency and operator support Willoughbeach Terrace's 50 units, address,
and new-construction classification. These three checks do not estimate a
national error rate.

Missing construction type creates a separate coverage limitation: 1,052 of
Indiana's 1,353 source records lack it (77.8\%), versus 3.1\% in California.
River Pointe in Tell City (INA20157173) is one excluded example; Indiana
describes a new building, and its developer reports opening it in 2015. Thus,
unknown construction type cannot be equated with rehabilitation. The previous
entry's statement that only repeated addresses and locations remain was too
narrow for a claim of complete national construction coverage.

The recommendation is to retain the simple default, document the identified
exceptions, review state counts most affected by repeated addresses, and assess
missing-type coverage by state and year. No source values or selected records
were changed by this search. The external findings are recorded for correction;
the current dataset still contains the Oconee Park conflict. Recent-year totals
also remain subject to HUD reporting revisions.

\paragraph{Reproduction.} Root \texttt{make} produces the report:
\newline
\path{tasks/audits/external_benchmarks/output/checks.txt}.\newline
The task's README
records source links, sample definitions, and individual findings. External
pages were read September 11, 2026. This audit adds no production data source.

\section*{Automatic selection and geographic coverage}
\noindent September 12, 2026. Agent-drafted research record; previous revision
\texttt{6fbe2c2}. This entry supersedes the earlier recommendations for manual
adjudication. No individual corrections enter production.

Jacob requested state maps and tables of construction-type completeness, a
comparison of all new-construction records with first addresses, and diagnostics
of geographic exclusions. Missing or inconsistent hedonics must not remove a
location. The selected dataset therefore retains earliest-year ties even when
names or hedonics disagree: the smallest HUD ID labels the address, all tied IDs
remain recorded, and each conflicting field becomes missing. Missing years at
repeated addresses still prevent ordering. Later records are omitted by the
first-address definition, without assuming that later phases are source errors.
This change adds 488 first-address choices relative to the earlier rule.

\begin{center}\small
\input{corroboration_summary.tex}
\end{center}

The confidence sample additionally requires a usable street address, a valid
1987--2024 placed-in-service year, and a checked location for every tied first
record. Census must match the state and agree with HUD within 500 meters; an exact
Census match suffices if HUD coordinates are absent. Scattered-site and
resyndication flags exclude records. A repeated address alone is not an exclusion.
Reported total units take priority over the bedroom breakdown; inconsistent
bedroom counts become missing. Source values remain untouched. The broad,
first-address, and confidence samples have 1,949,494, 1,862,606, and 1,435,150
reported units, respectively; missing unit counts are omitted from these sums.

The first-address rule removes 4.3\% of source new-construction records. Confidence
checks remove a further 24.2\% of first-address records, with large state differences:
88.2\% in Kentucky, 43.7\% in Minnesota, and 6.4\% in Oregon. Kentucky loses 87
first-address records to unusable addresses, usually a street without a number.
Minnesota loses 222 to unusable addresses, often text indicating multiple sites.
Applying national retention rates within reported-year groups to each state's own
year mix still leaves excess losses of 68.1 percentage points in Kentucky and
20.7 in Minnesota. This descriptive adjustment does not establish representativeness.

Missing construction type affects 7,071 of 54,928 source records in the 50 states
and DC (12.9\%). Indiana's missing share is 77.8\%, compared with California's
3.1\%. The automatic exclusions are therefore geographically systematic. No
state-specific exceptions, weighting, or manual review queue is introduced.
The documented Oconee Park classification conflict remains without an ID-specific
override: passing these screens does not certify every source classification.

\section*{Use HUD coordinates and make source acquisition visible}
\noindent September 12, 2026. Agent-drafted research record; previous revision
\texttt{93e5f9a}. This entry replaces the requirement for universal Census
corroboration in the preceding entry.

HUD supplies coordinates for 99 of Kentucky's 102 new-construction records.
The prior rule excluded 87 records because their addresses were unsuitable for
Census queries, even though 85 of those records already had HUD coordinates.
Jacob questioned that restriction and directed the use of HUD coordinates when
available. The dataset now uses valid HUD coordinates by default, retaining
Census comparisons as diagnostics. An exact Census address match in the reported
state supplies a fallback only when HUD coordinates are absent. A street without
a house number no longer invalidates an available HUD point.

\begin{center}\small
\input{../tasks/build_lihtc/output/summary.tex}
\end{center}

The first-address sample remains 28,196 records. The final sample increases by
4,452 to 25,832 records, with 1,730,065 reported units. It contains 25,763 HUD
points and 69 Census fallback points. Missing hedonics remain permissible: 249
retained records lack total units and 6,469 lack a consistent bedroom breakdown.
The data preserve 1,310 retained HUD--Census disagreements above 500 meters;
that threshold now describes a comparison, not an exclusion. Two tied earliest
address groups have conflicting selected coordinates and remain excluded.

Kentucky increases from 12 to 94 retained records; Minnesota increases from 391
to 678. Kentucky's remaining exclusions are one missing year, two unavailable
locations, four scattered-site flags, and one resyndication flag, counted in
that priority order. Unresolved addresses with available points retain their
HUD IDs; they have not been established as distinct physical addresses. The
missing construction-type problem is unchanged, and the retained sample should
still be described as a selected sample rather than complete national coverage.

Source acquisition is now implemented by short named shell scripts. Each task
Makefile declares the actual source target, input symlink, and output producer;
only generic.make and shell\_functions.make remain as shared Make includes.
Dataset metadata reports are saved alongside their datasets through SaveData,
not represented as separate Make targets. The root retains the explicit
cross-task dependency graph. The revision follows the explicit input/output
pattern in the Gottlieb Saiz task and the hclose task discussion, without
importing older download includes or report-target machinery.

\paragraph{Reproduction.} Root \texttt{make} rebuilds the data, diagnostics, and
this logbook; \texttt{make} in paper/ checks the data and compiles the paper.
The pinned HUD workbook and archived Census responses have not changed. Existing
source snapshots are not refreshed when acquisition implementation changes;
source-vintage changes require an explicit new target and checksum.

\section*{Lock the project-location dataset before exploration}
\noindent September 12, 2026. Agent-drafted research record; previous revision
\texttt{3bba0c8}.\newline
Release tag: \texttt{lihtc-v1.0.0}.

Jacob requested a finalized dataset with fixed decisions and readable code before
viewing further exploratory results. Version 1.0.0 freezes the existing
first-address and HUD-coordinate rules. It contains 25,832 dated project
locations: 25,763 HUD points and 69 exact Census fallback points. A companion
file preserves all 28,196 first-address choices, including excluded and undated
records. Missing hedonics do not exclude projects: the dated file retains 249
without total units and 6,469 without a complete consistent bedroom breakdown.
Its reported-unit sum is 1,730,065, omitting missing counts from that sum.

The 997 source new-construction records without valid HUD coordinates do not
yield roughly 427 recovered locations. That number counts addresses suitable
for submitting a query. The archived responses supply only 72 accepted exact
matches among those 997 records; 69 survive first-address and final-sample rules.
The remaining source records comprise 570 unqueryable addresses, 228 unmatched
queries, 10 tied matches, and 117 non-exact matches. HUD coordinates account for
nearly all of the final sample's location coverage.

The release README fixes the source vintage, address normalization, date window,
earliest-year ordering, automatic tie handling, location source priority,
scattered-site and resyndication exclusions, and treatment of missing hedonics.
The variable dictionary specifies all 41 columns, including source category
codes. Reading the pinned HUD dictionary identified a labeling problem:
\texttt{N\_4BR} is described as four-bedroom units. The derived column is now
\texttt{bedrooms\_4}, replacing \texttt{bedrooms\_4plus} without changing any
values. The main R scripts now expose the transformations in numbered, linear
blocks with visible reads and writes. A comparison with the previous outputs
found identical rows, IDs, values, and ordering after that one column rename.

This freeze does not resolve source coverage limitations. The dated file retains
882 records whose address identity is unresolved and 1,310 HUD--Census distance
disagreements above 500 meters. Missing construction type and other exclusions
vary across states; the known external Oconee Park classification conflict remains
documented without an individual override. The release is a fixed selected sample,
not a complete census of construction or verified building footprints.

\paragraph{Reproduction.} The release lives in \path{releases/v1.0.0/}.
It includes exact copies of the two selected datasets and their producer reports,
source/output SHA-256 fingerprints, and recorded runtime versions. Root
\texttt{make} writes task outputs and cannot overwrite this snapshot. Reproduction
checks compare those outputs with the frozen files. Further analyses must read
this version; changing a source or selection rule requires a new release and a
documented reason. No new exploratory plots were introduced for the freeze.
